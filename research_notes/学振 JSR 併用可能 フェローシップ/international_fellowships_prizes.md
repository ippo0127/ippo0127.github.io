# International (non-Japanese-funded) fellowships, awards and prizes for a UTokyo PhD student in hep-th / mathematical physics, and compatibility with JSPS DC and the UTokyo Physics JSR Fellowship

Research date: 2026-11-12. Applicant profile: Japanese national, PhD student at UTokyo Physics (Kavli IPMU), hep-th / mathematical physics (TQFT), staying enrolled in Japan.

Baseline compatibility rule used throughout (from the assignment background, not re-verified here): the JSPS DC FAQ allows 公募 grants/scholarships from 民間企業等 as long as the research-dedication duty (研究専念義務) is respected, and allows prizes with no restriction on use. The JSR Fellowship's own concurrency rules were not examined in this research (see Gaps).

Classification key: **Compatible** = eligible from Japan and no exclusivity clause that hits DC/JSR. **Conditional** = eligible on paper but blocked or constrained by field fit, nomination, internship, or an unverified rule. **Incompatible** = the program's own rules conflict with DC/JSR. **Not eligible** = UTokyo students cannot apply (institution location, invite list, career stage).

## Q1. Corporate PhD fellowships: are UTokyo students eligible, and do they forbid other funding?

### Takeaway
None of the corporate fellowships is a realistic fit for hep-th/TQFT. Google and Microsoft accept Japan but fund CS/AI topics only. Jane Street does accept physics, but only at institutions in the US, Canada, UK, Europe, Australia, New Zealand or Singapore, so a UTokyo student cannot apply. Google's exclusivity clause names only "comparable industry awards" and exempts government funding, so DC would not block it if the field fit existed.

### Cited Findings

**Google PhD Fellowship** — Conditional (field mismatch; needs UTokyo nomination)
- Regions include "East Asia". East Asia award: US$10K for 1 year. Students cannot apply directly; the university nominates (up to 3 per university for East Asia). — [Google Research PhD Fellowship](https://research.google/programs-and-events/phd-fellowship/)
- Research areas: Computer Architecture; Algorithms and Optimization; Health; HCI; ML and ML Foundations; Machine Perception; NLP; Privacy/Safety/Security; **Quantum Computing** (quantum algorithms, quantum error correction, early fault-tolerant computing, NISQ, superconducting qubits, classical simulation of quantum algorithms); Software Engineering/PL; Systems/Networking. Physics and mathematics are not listed. — [Google Research PhD Fellowship](https://research.google/programs-and-events/phd-fellowship/)
- 2026 cycle: open Mar 5, 2026, close Apr 30, 2026, recipients announced by Oct 31, 2026. No 2027 dates posted yet. — [Google Research PhD Fellowship](https://research.google/programs-and-events/phd-fellowship/)
- Exclusivity clause: students "already supported by a comparable industry award are not eligible"; government or non-profit funding is exempt. Recipients must "remain enrolled full-time in the PhD program for the duration of the Fellowship or forfeit the award." East Asia applicants must have completed most or all graduate coursework by the award year. — [Google Research PhD Fellowship](https://research.google/programs-and-events/phd-fellowship/)
- An older (2024-cycle) Google page, quoted in search snippets, said universities in Japan, Korea and Taiwan may nominate up to three students. The current page says only "East Asia" and does not list countries. — search summary of [google.cn PhD Fellowship page](https://google.cn/intl/en_cn/university/research/phdfellowship) (not directly verified; the FAQ URL `research.google/programs-and-events/phd-fellowship/faq/` returned 404)

**Microsoft Research Fellowship (successor to the MSR Asia Fellowship)** — Conditional, effectively not eligible (no physics/math challenge)
- Japan is an eligible region. The award for Japan, Hong Kong, Korea, Singapore and Taiwan is US$17,000, which "include[s] travel funding". It is paid as an "unrestricted gift" in one payment "directly to the institution/university", never to individuals. PhD students may apply themselves. A background screen is required. — [Microsoft Research Fellowship](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/microsoft-research-fellowship/)
- The page has no clause about other funding or exclusivity. — [Microsoft Research Fellowship](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/microsoft-research-fellowship/)
- The 2026 call had 17 research challenges, all AI/robotics/genomics/energy. None was in quantum, physics or mathematics. The call shows as "Closed November 2025", and no 2027 call was found. — [2026 Research Challenges](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/2026-microsoft-research-fellowship/research-challenges/)
- Secondary sources gave a 2026 deadline of December 2, 2025. — [grantsdatabase.org](https://grantsdatabase.org/2025/11/06/microsoft-research-fellowship-2026/)

**Jane Street Graduate Research Fellowship** — Not eligible (institution location)
- Fields: EECS, mathematics, **physics**, statistics, or closely related fields. Research need not relate to finance. — [Jane Street GRF](https://www.janestreet.com/join-jane-street/programs-and-events/graduate-research-fellowship/)
- Key restriction: the fellow must stay a full-time PhD student "at an accredited research institution in the US, Canada, UK, Europe, Australia, New Zealand, or Singapore". International students are eligible, but Japan is not on the institution list. — [Jane Street GRF](https://www.janestreet.com/join-jane-street/programs-and-events/graduate-research-fellowship/)
- Award: full tuition and fees plus a US$50,000 stipend for one academic year. Finalists get US$5,000. — [Jane Street GRF](https://www.janestreet.com/join-jane-street/programs-and-events/graduate-research-fellowship/)
- 2027 cycle: student applications due Nov 13, 2026 (AoE), letters due Dec 1, 2026, notifications in February. — [Jane Street GRF](https://www.janestreet.com/join-jane-street/programs-and-events/graduate-research-fellowship/)
- Other funding: "Existing funding does not exclude you". The total stipend will likely rise, but not by a full extra $50,000; the final amount depends on university rules and caps. — [Jane Street GRF](https://www.janestreet.com/join-jane-street/programs-and-events/graduate-research-fellowship/)

**NVIDIA Graduate Fellowship** — Not eligible in practice (CS fields) and likely incompatible (mandatory internship)
- Fields: CS, computer engineering, system architecture, EE or related. International PhD students may apply. Awards are up to US$60,000, paid to the university. A mandatory in-person internship at an NVIDIA office in summer 2027 precedes the award year. Locations are US, Canada, Germany, Sweden, Finland, Switzerland, Israel, Taiwan and Singapore (not Japan). The 2027–28 deadline was Oct 30, 2026, which has passed. — [NVIDIA Graduate Fellowship](https://research.nvidia.com/graduate-fellowships); [NVIDIA blog 2027–28 call](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/applications-open-graduate-fellowship-awards-2026)

**Apple Scholars in AI/ML** — Not eligible (invited universities only; AI/ML)
- Invitation only; each invited university nominates up to 3 students. Students "cannot be supported ... by another industry-sponsored full fellowship". Any nationality may apply. The 2026 nomination window closed Sep 8, 2026. Topics are AI/ML (privacy-preserving ML, human-centered AI, etc.). — search summary of university nomination pages, e.g. [UMD Apple cover sheet](https://gradschool.umd.edu/sites/default/files/2023-08/APPLE%20COVER%20SHEET_V2.pdf), [Oxford MPLS](https://www.mpls.ox.ac.uk/graduate-school/funding-for-graduate-students/apple-scholars-in-ai-ml)

**Meta Research PhD Fellowship** — Not eligible in practice (CS/AI areas). Aggregator-only sources.
- Aggregators describe: full-time PhD students worldwide in Meta's announced areas; two years of tuition plus a US$42,000 stipend; 2027-cycle deadline around Sep 2026 (dates conflict between sources). Meta's official page was not retrieved. — [afterschoolafrica](https://www.afterschoolafrica.com/75409/meta-research-phd-fellowship/)

**Bloomberg Data Science PhD Fellowship** — Not eligible in practice (data science/ML)
- 2027–28 applications were due Sep 25, 2026. Full-time doctoral enrollment is required, and the fellowship is renewable for up to 3 years. The official page could not be read, and no country-of-institution rule was confirmed. — [Purdue listing](https://engineering.purdue.edu/AAE/academics/graduate/Announcements/funding-FA26/FA26-bloomberg_data_science_phd_fellowship); [Bloomberg official page](https://www.bloomberg.com/company/values/tech-at-bloomberg/data-science/academic-engagement-programs/data-science-ph-d-fellowship)

**Qualcomm Innovation Fellowship** — Not eligible in practice (engineering/CS; listed universities only)
- Regional programs exist for North America, Europe, South Korea, East Asia and India, each limited to listed universities. East Asia 2026 teams applied by Aug 31, 2026. Whether UTokyo is on the East Asia list was not verified. — [Qualcomm QIF 2026 East Asia](https://www.qualcomm.com/research/university-relations/innovation-fellowship/2026-east-asia); [Qualcomm QIF](https://www.qualcomm.com/innovationfellowship)

**IBM PhD Fellowship** — Status unknown. Only an aggregator "forecasted" date (Mar 15, 2027) was found, and nothing official. — search result only, not verified

### Inferences
- **Google** is the only corporate fellowship whose own text clearly allows government money alongside it ("government or non-profit funding is exempt"). DC is government money and JSR is university money, so neither trips the clause. On the JSPS side, a Google award is a 公募 grant from a 民間企業, which the DC FAQ allows. It is blocked only by field: TQFT would qualify only if framed as quantum computing (topological quantum computation, quantum error correction). It also requires a UTokyo internal nomination. If attempted, the 2027 window is likely Mar–Apr 2027, by analogy with 2026 (inferred, not announced).
- **Microsoft** pays to the university as an unrestricted gift. This would probably be handled as a 寄附金 to the lab rather than personal income, but the challenges are AI-only.
- **Jane Street** is the most attractive on paper (accepts physics, explicitly allows existing funding), but it is closed to UTokyo enrollment. It becomes relevant only if the student enrolls at an institution in the listed regions.
- **NVIDIA's** mandatory overseas internship would raise a DC research-dedication (専念義務) question even if the field fit.

### Gaps
- Google's current list of East Asia countries (the FAQ URL returned 404), and whether UTokyo runs an internal nomination for the Google PhD Fellowship.
- Official Meta, Bloomberg, IBM, Two Sigma, Hudson River Trading, Citadel, Amazon, Quantinuum and Google Quantum AI fellowship pages were not reached. All are expected to be CS/quant/AI-focused and mostly US-institution-based, but this is unverified.
- Microsoft Research Fellowship 2027 call: not found.

## Q2. Foundation and postdoc-type programs (Schmidt, Breakthrough, Simons, Kavli, Templeton, Humboldt, Rhodes, etc.)

### Takeaway
Of the foundation programs checked, none funds a PhD student who stays enrolled in Japan. Schmidt Science Fellows and Breakthrough New Horizons are post-PhD. They matter at graduation, not now.

### Cited Findings
- **Schmidt Science Fellows**: postdoctoral; requires nomination by the PhD-awarding institution, which must be a Nominating Partner. For 2027 the PhD completion window was May 1, 2026–Jun 30, 2027. Stipend US$110,000/yr for up to 2 years, for postdoc study in a field different from the PhD. — [MIT page](https://research.mit.edu/research-development/postdoctoral-fellowships/schmidt-science-fellowship); [Schmidt Who Can Apply](https://schmidtsciencefellows.org/selection/who-can-apply/)
- Tohoku University was a Nominating Partner for the 2027 cohort, recommending 2 candidates internally; the official deadlines fell in May–Jul 2026. — [Tohoku notice (PDF, Japanese)](https://www.lifesci.tohoku.ac.jp/media/files/_u/topic/file/2g9e3pucx7.pdf)
- **Breakthrough Prize, New Horizons in Physics**: the official rules state no age restriction. A Cambridge listing says recipients are generally no more than 12 years past the PhD. Self-nomination is not allowed. The 2027-cycle public nominations ran Apr 22–Jul 17, 2026. A new **Vera Rubin New Frontiers Prize** (US$50,000) is for women within two years of completing the PhD. — [Breakthrough Prize news](https://breakthroughprize.org/News/100); [Breakthrough rules](https://breakthroughprize.org/Rules/1); [Cambridge funding listing](https://funding.phy.cam.ac.uk/171/)

### Inferences
- Schmidt: whether UTokyo is a Nominating Partner is unknown. It is relevant only for the year the PhD is completed, and it requires switching fields.
- New Horizons and New Frontiers are designed for people who already hold a PhD, so they are not realistic while enrolled.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session: Simons Foundation, Kavli Foundation and Templeton (none is known to run open individual PhD-student fellowships for Japan-enrolled students); Humboldt (postdoc); Rhodes, Gates and Marshall (require enrolling in the UK, so out of scope); L'Oréal–UNESCO For Women in Science (gender-specific; the Japan national program is run by 日本ロレアル); IUPAP Early Career Scientist Prize and ICTP prizes (believed to be post-PhD); APCTP; String Math / Strings student travel support. These are listed from general knowledge only and need checking before use.

## Q3. Prizes and essay/paper competitions open to PhD students (no use restriction, so compatible with DC)

### Takeaway
Prizes are the cleanest compatible option: they carry no exclusivity, and the DC FAQ allows prizes. Concrete open calls are the Gravity Research Foundation essays (deadline Mar 31, 2027) and the Buchalter Cosmology Prize (PhD students explicitly eligible; window Oct 1, 2026–Sep 30, 2027; cosmology scope). The ICBS Frontiers of Science Award is nomination-based, and the IAMP Early Career Award (under 35) is theoretically open but realistically aimed at established researchers.

### Cited Findings
- **Gravity Research Foundation Awards for Essays on Gravitation (2027)**: deadline Mar 31, 2027; awards announced May 15, 2027; five cash awards (aggregator lists $4,000 / $700 / $600 / $500 / $400); one essay per author; no AI-generated essays. Winners are published in a special issue of IJMPD. — [GRF official](https://www.gravityresearchfoundation.org/competition); [Atom Grants listing](https://atomgrants.com/grant/gravity-research-foundation-2027-essays-on-gravitation)
- **Buchalter Cosmology Prize (2027 prize)**: open to people who hold a PhD in physics/astrophysics/astronomy or a closely related field, **or are "currently in an accredited graduate program pursuing a PhD"** in those fields. No nationality or institution restriction is stated. The paper must be on arXiv with an original date no earlier than Jan 1, 2025. Submissions are accepted Oct 1, 2026–Sep 30, 2027, up to 2 per submitter. Scope is original cosmology research going beyond the standard model and paradigms. — [Buchalter Submissions](https://www.buchaltercosmologyprize.org/submissions)
- Buchalter prizes: $10,000 / $5,000 / $2,500, "split equally among the authors". The 2027 winners will be announced in January 2028. — [Buchalter Prize Details](https://www.buchaltercosmologyprize.org/prize-details)
- **FQxI Essay Competition**: the latest found is the 13th, "How Quantum is Life?" (US$53,000 pool), with winners announced Feb 14, 2026. No new 2026–27 contest was found. — [FQxI competitions](https://qspace.fqxi.org/competitions/home); [EurekAlert release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1116620)
- **ICBS Frontiers of Science Award** (International Congress of Basic Science, Beijing): covers papers in mathematics, theoretical physics, and theoretical CS/information science, with emphasis on results from the past five years. Nominations and reviews come from invited scholars (no open application). 148 papers were recognized in 2025. — [U Toronto math news](https://www.mathematics.utoronto.ca/node/2539); [ICBS 2023 award page](https://2023.icbs.cn/en/web/index/18009_1581229); [IHES report](https://www.ihes.fr/?p=41966)
- **IAMP Early Career Award**: for a single achievement in mathematical physics by a researcher under 35 (age on July 31 of the Congress year). It is presented at the triennial ICMP; past winners include Dyatlov (2018), Aggarwal (2021) and Hintz (2024). — [Wikipedia: IAMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Mathematical_Physics) (iamp.org page content could not be extracted)

### Inferences
- GRF and Buchalter are realistic self-entry options. Buchalter needs a cosmology-relevant paper; GRF needs a short gravitation essay. Prize money has no use restriction, so it fits the DC FAQ's prize allowance and does not conflict with JSR (assuming JSR has no income-reporting rule; see Gaps).
- ICBS awards can come to student co-authors of nominated papers, but there is no way to apply.
- The next ICMP is presumably 2027 (triennial after 2024), so IAMP ECA nominations would be around 2027. This is inferred and the date is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- Official GRF 2027 eligibility text (whether students are explicitly eligible) was not retrieved, only the deadline and award count.
- ICMP 2027 location/dates and the IAMP nomination deadline: not retrieved.
- Whether ICBS prize money is paid to each student co-author: not found.

## Q4. Funded meetings for young researchers (HLF, Lindau)

### Takeaway
The Heidelberg Laureate Forum is open to PhD students in mathematics "or closely related fields". Board and lodging are covered, and a travel grant is available only after one's own institution or funder refuses. The 2027 application window is open now (Nov 11, 2026–Feb 11, 2027). Lindau's physics meeting is in 2028; Japanese participants go through JSPS nomination, so that route is Japanese-funded.

### Cited Findings
- **14th HLF**: Sep 12–17, 2027, Heidelberg. Applications Nov 11, 2026–Feb 11, 2027 (11:59 pm Berlin). Open to undergraduates, PhD students and postdocs within 5 years of the PhD in "mathematics and computer science (or closely related fields)"; one attendance per academic level. Selection by reviewers appointed by ACM, DNVA and IMU; top 100 per discipline; notification end of April 2027. — [HLF FAQ](https://www.heidelberg-laureate-forum.org/young-researchers/faq/); [HLF poster via IMU](https://www.mathunion.org/fileadmin/documents/2026-08/HLFF_DinA0_Poster.pdf)
- HLF costs: the HLFF books and pays a single room with breakfast (Sep 11–18) plus meals at the venue. Travel is not automatically covered; apply first to your own institution or national funder, and only on refusal apply for an HLFF travel grant (rejection letter required; window May 3–Jun 23, 2027). — [HLF FAQ](https://www.heidelberg-laureate-forum.org/young-researchers/faq/)
- **Lindau**: the 76th meeting (Jun 27–Jul 2, 2027) is Physiology/Medicine, and JSPS applications for it closed Aug 3, 2026. — [JSPS Lindau](https://www.jsps.go.jp/english/e-lindau/index.html)
- Lindau rotation: the 77th meeting in 2028 is physics. — [OeAW Lindau page](https://stipendien.oeaw.ac.at/en/lindau-nobel-laureate-meetings/lindau-nobel-laureate-meetings/)

### Inferences
- HLF: a TQFT/mathematical-physics student should state the mathematical content and may ask yr@heidelberg-laureate-forum.org whether the field counts as "closely related" to mathematics. DC holders could pay travel from 特別研究員奨励費 (科研費), which is the "own funder" route HLF expects. Participation is not a fellowship, so there is no conflict.
- Lindau 2028 (physics): the JSPS call would likely open around summer 2027 (inferred from the 2027 cycle's Aug 3, 2026 deadline). This is JSPS-funded, so it falls outside the strict "non-Japanese funder" scope, but it is the only route for Japanese applicants.

### Gaps
- JSPS Lindau eligibility details (career stage, whether DC fellows are eligible, coverage): the English page lacked them; see the Japanese 募集要項.
- Amount of the HLFF travel grant: not stated.

### Takeaway
For a hep-th/TQFT student enrolled in Japan, no international *fellowship* (stipend-type) was found that is both open to Japan-enrolled students and in-field. Jane Street is in-field but excludes Japanese institutions; Google and Microsoft accept Japan but are CS/AI. The compatible options are prizes (GRF, Buchalter, possibly FQxI or ICBS) and funded participation (HLF 2027), none of which conflicts with DC or JSR.

### Inferences
- Under the DC FAQ (民間企業等の公募の助成・奨学金 allowed within 専念義務; prizes allowed), every non-excluded item above is compatible from the JSPS side. Programs with internships (NVIDIA) or exclusivity against industry fellowships (Apple; Google, for industry awards only) are the only clauses that bite, and none of them targets government fellowships such as DC.
